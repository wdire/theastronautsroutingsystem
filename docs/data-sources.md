## Notes

- **Close-up ground detail (ambientCG Ground079S, Ground110, Rock062).** Shape only: their surface normals, height and brightness add grain below the 25 cm of HiRISE within a few tens of metres of the viewer in Rover View. They are photographs of Earth materials, drawn in, not measured; the colour comes from the orbital data.
- **CRISM targeted observations of Jezero, CR2 browse composite.** Spectral overlay, zoom 9 to 12: red, green, blue are the summary parameters MIN2295_2480, MIN2345_2537, CINDEX2; carbonate indicators: magnesium carbonate shows yellow to white. Each image keeps the CRISM team's own stretch, so colours are not comparable between images. Opacity follows signal strength (our choice, to let the map show through); a faint tint marks each image's footprint. Found through the PDS Orbital Data Explorer. Images cover 13% of the tile area.
- **CRISM targeted observations of Jezero, MAF browse composite.** Spectral overlay, zoom 9 to 12: red, green, blue are the summary parameters OLINDEX3, LCPINDEX2, HCPINDEX2; olivine (red), low-calcium pyroxene (green), high-calcium pyroxene (blue). Each image keeps the CRISM team's own stretch, so colours are not comparable between images. Opacity follows signal strength (our choice, to let the map show through); a faint tint marks each image's footprint. Found through the PDS Orbital Data Explorer. Images cover 13% of the tile area.
- **CTX global mosaic (Murray Lab beta01), about 5 m.** Fine detail of the core imagery at zoom 11 to 13 (high-pass only), and at zoom 14 where the Mars 2020 ortho has no data. About one tile in six on the server is cut short and holds the wrong picture; those are rebuilt from the level below, or enlarged from the level above. The app also reads it live outside the package.
- **Curiosity REMS daily weather at Gale crater.** The last 30 sols and the lowest and highest values of the whole feed (sols 1 to 4995, last dated 2026-08-25). Air temperature in C, pressure in Pa. Values outside -140 to 40 C or 500 to 1200 Pa are feed glitches and are dropped. Gale crater is not Jezero: shown as a measured reference beside the modelled values.
- **Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature: Mars feature names.** Downloaded from https://asc-planetarynames-data.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/MARS_nomenclature_center_pts.zip. Kept: approved names with their centre within 2 degrees of the region, and features over 300 km across that reach into it from nearby. Albedo features are left out. minZoom is set from the feature's size, so large features are labelled first.
- **Geologic Map of Mars (USGS SIM 3292).** Queried from https://trek.nasa.gov/mars/trekarcgis/rest/services/SIM3292_Global_Geology/MapServer/0/query for the region envelope, clipped to it, borders simplified to about 1 km. Colours are the USGS map colours from the layer's renderer. The map was made at 1:20,000,000, so unit borders are good to a few kilometres at best. The one-line descriptions, periods and ages in words are plain-English summaries of the map's unit descriptions; ages follow the period boundaries of Michael (2013): Noachian before about 3.7 billion years ago, Hesperian to about 3.4, Amazonian since.
- **Habitat Demonstration Unit (part 1) 3D model.** The base beside the start of a route. Modelled in centimetres: drawn at scale 0.03. Optimised with gltf-transform: meshopt compression, 1024 px WebP textures, meshes simplified by half.
- **Ingenuity flight paths and landing points.** From https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/M20/Layers/json/m20_heli_flight_path.json and https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/M20/Layers/json/m20_heli_waypoints.json. 72 flights, 18.139 km in all. The flight paths carry no heights: each leg is given the ground height of its landing point.
- **Ingenuity Mars Helicopter 3D model.** Helicopter in the visor. Optimised with gltf-transform: meshopt compression, 1024 px WebP textures, meshes simplified by half.
- **Live space weather: CMEs modelled to Mars, particle events, solar flares.** Asked live, at most every 30 minutes, through api/spaceweather.ts (api.nasa.gov, with ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov as the fallback for each feed) or by the browser from ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov directly. Not bundled. Arrival times at Mars come from the WSA-ENLIL model and are good to about half a day. A slice retrieved on 2026-11-14 is kept in tests/fixtures/donki-2026-11.json for the unit tests.
- **Mars 2020 named places.** Each place sits at the rover's localised stop on the sol it worked there (the two airfields at Ingenuity's landing points). These are the team's informal names, not IAU names. - **Mars 2020 Perseverance Rover 3D model.** Robotic rover in the visor; its six wheels turn as it drives. Optimised with gltf-transform: meshopt compression, 2048 px WebP textures, no mesh simplification.
- **Mars 2020 terrain relative navigation CTX DTM mosaic of Jezero, 20 m.** Crater elevation, zoom 12 and 13. Level-matched to the HRSC and MOLA blend (its long wavelengths follow the blend) and feathered into it at the edge of the model.
- **Mars 2020 terrain relative navigation CTX orthomosaic of Jezero, 6 m.** Fine detail of the crater imagery at zoom 14 (high-pass only).
- **Mars 2020 terrain relative navigation HiRISE DTM mosaic of Jezero, 1 m.** Elevation at zoom 14, read at 5 m by HTTP ranges (every fifth row, columns averaged in fives). Level-matched to the CTX model beneath it and feathered at its edge. Also read live at full resolution by /api/hirise (HTTP ranges, one per row, cut into 512 px tiles and cached for a year): Rover View's close and near ground, and the map's terrain at zoom 15 and 16. Levelled onto the package by the median difference over each patch or tile.
- **Mars 2020 terrain relative navigation HiRISE orthomosaic of Jezero, 25 cm.** Read live by /api/hirise from USGS's copy on Amazon S3 (HTTP ranges, one per row; 25 cm to 8 m in six levels, 512 px tiles, cached for a year). One band (HiRISE RED): its brightness, as a ratio to its own local mean, is laid over the orbital colour, in Rover View within 2 km of the viewer and on the map from zoom 15. Not in the package.
- **Mars Global Cave Candidate Catalog (MGC3).** 1062 candidate cave entrances on Mars. 0 lie inside the region (13.1 to 23.8 N, 71.8 to 83.1 E); the file keeps the 3 within 6 degrees of it, each marked inRegion false. They are candidates seen in orbital images, not confirmed caves. Priority 1 is the catalog's highest targeting priority.
- **Mars HRSC and MOLA blended digital elevation model, 200 m, version 2.** Region elevation, zoom 6 to 10, read by HTTP ranges for the window only. Metres above the MOLA areoid.
- **Mars landing sites (curated list).** The ten landers and rovers that landed and worked on Mars, with landing date (UTC), place and a one-line note taken from the NASA GISS list. Missions that failed on landing are left out, as is Mars 3, which fell silent about 20 seconds after landing. Coordinates are from the same list, except: Viking 2 (47.64 N, 134.29 E) and Mars Pathfinder (19.13 N, 326.78 E) use planetocentric values, because the list gives planetographic latitudes for them; Perseverance uses its first localisation in JPL's MMGIS waypoints. Most sites lie outside the app's region on purpose.
- **MOLA Mission Experiment Gridded Data Record, topography at 16 pixels per degree.** Planet elevation tiles, zoom 0 to 3. Heights are metres above the MOLA areoid.
- **Perseverance MEDA daily weather at Jezero crater.** The feed holds 7 sols and has not been updated since 2024-04-27 (sol 1133). Kept as it is, with its dates.
- **Perseverance raw images (Navcam and Mastcam-Z).** From https://mars.nasa.gov/rss/api/?feed=raw_images&category=mars2020&feedtype=json . One to three frames per place, taken while the rover was parked there, resized to 1200 px wide WebP. Raw frames: colours are as the camera recorded them, not calibrated. Each photo keeps its NASA image id and link.
- **Perseverance sample list.** 30 samples as published by NASA. Each is placed at the rover's localised stop (JPL MMGIS waypoints) on the sol it was taken. NASA lists the sol a tube was sealed; the coring sol is read from the raw images feed (the last sol up to sealing with a full set of CacheCam frames), because a few tubes were sealed later and elsewhere. NASA gives no sealing sol yet for samples 29 and 30 (Bell Island, Gallants): their sols come from the photo captions on the same page (sols 1497 and 1551) and the CacheCam frames around them. The campaign is derived from NASA's sampling location.
- **Perseverance traverse and localised waypoints.** From https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/M20/Layers/json/M20_traverse.json and https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/M20/Layers/json/M20_waypoints.json. The drive is thinned to one point every 2 m, and segments the source repeats for drives that ran over several sols are kept once, so the drawn line is a little shorter than the rover's odometry in the waypoints. Heights are metres above the Mars areoid. Covers sols 13 to 2022 (45.246 km). The app asks /api/traverse for the current track first (the same steps, cached for six hours) and uses this copy when that does not answer.
- **Space Exploration Vehicle 3D model.** Pressurised rover in the visor; its six wheels turn as it drives. Optimised with gltf-transform: meshopt compression, 2048 px WebP textures, no mesh simplification.
- **THEMIS daytime infrared global mosaic, 100 m, version 2.** Fine detail of the region imagery at zoom 9 and 10: only its high-pass is used, on Viking colour.
- **Viking MDIM 2.1 colour mosaic, 232 m.** Planet imagery, zoom 0 to 4, and the colour of the region, zoom 5 to 8. The hue is pulled toward ochre with the luminance kept and the brightest highlights rolled off (polar ice stays white); that grading is ours, not the product's.
- **Z2 Spacesuit 3D model.** The suited walkers. Not simplified: thin parts tear. Optimised with gltf-transform: meshopt compression, 1024 px WebP textures, no mesh simplification.
